Did the offline rank predict the arm?

EX-4 / AEP Q3 · 10,000 permutations

Best vs worst fidelity on placement (AEP Q1, E2): −0.84 px, 97.5% CI [−2.53, +0.88], tolerance-compatible with the 5 px tolerance. On success (E1) the ceiling rule applies, so the decision is indeterminate. The AEP claim is not falsified here.

Engine cost on the deployment target

trtexec · tegrastats 100 ms · power_bench/SUMMARY.md

FP16 is the fastest and the cheapest per inference. INT8 draws about 1 W less while it runs but takes 45% longer, so each inference costs more. The three INT8 calibrations cost the same.

End to end in the trial runner, including preprocessing and the socket round trip, the same order holds: FP16 24.7 ms, INT8 34.3 ms and FP32 79.0 ms (FP16 block). On the real arm FP16 succeeded 19/20, with placement within tolerance of FP32.

What moved the success rate

G6 + public stress tallies

The same arm and the same policy. Between precisions no success difference was detected. Grip friction and a 3 cm shift moved a lot. The gripper and shift blocks are separate sessions, so read them as observations, not as a randomized comparison.
